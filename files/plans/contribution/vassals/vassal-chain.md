## The layers — the dev, 15/11: *"model the base information correctly, then this, then that — think of SOLID"*

Each layer does one thing; a layer is added without touching the ones below. The coder designs the types.

| layer | carries | principle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0 — base** | what every record has: a name (localized), a Wikidata id, a period of existence with its calendar | single responsibility: *who it is* |
| **1 — kind** | a **person** adds birth and death; a **house** its founder; a **place** its position | open to extension: the base is not modified, the kind extends it |
| **2 — claims** | separate records of one shape — who, what, when, source lines, contrary readings kept: a **title** held, a **link** of vassalage, a **kinship**, a **confession**, a **role** in an event | open/closed: a new kind of claim (an alliance, an embassy) is added without touching layers 0–1; interface segregation: a screen reads only the claim it needs |
| **3 — derived** | the chain up to the suzerain, the succession of a title, the family tree, the certainty of a link | never entered, computed from layer 2: nothing retyped, nothing to drift |

So a confession, a rank or a successor is never a field of a sheet: a claim of layer 2, or a computation of layer 3.

## The model

| record | says | example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **person** (`people`, « Personnes » — `actors` renamed and extended) | **who someone is**, nothing more: name, Wikidata id, birth and death, house | Gilles de Rais |
| **title** | what a person holds or exercises **at a date**: a territory (the county of Nantes) or an office (marshal of France), with its source lines | Gilles de Rais, lord of Tiffauges; marshal of France from 1429 |
| **link** (*lien d'hommage*) | a person **for a title** is the man of another person **for a title**, over a period, with its source lines; a divergent reading is a second link | Robert II, for Flanders, of Philip I — and, for Imperial Flanders, of Henry IV |
| **sheet** of a place | its holder is a **person for a title** — the first link of the chain | Nantes in 1100 → Mathias II, count of Nantes |

**Four rules**, each against a case that breaks without it:

1. **A link names the title it is for**, never the person alone — Robert II of Flanders answers to two lords for two
   lands in 1100; a person-to-person link would make that a contradiction.
2. **A link is recorded once** and read by every sheet that goes through it. The form keeps the dev's gesture: on
   choosing « Duc de Bretagne », it shows the chain already known and sourced, and ⊕ is used only where it stops.
   Entered per sheet, « Brittany → France » would be retyped on Nantes, Rennes, Vannes… and drift (the dev's rule of
   14/11: each link proved once per date).
3. **The person's sheet holds no rank, no role and no confession.** A confession changes too — Charles II, Anglican, then Catholic on his deathbed (Wikidata gives both, undated) — so it is a dated claim. Pascal has no rank; Gilles de Rais has two, at two dates. Rank
   goes to the title, a part in an event to the event (`events.actors`), so one register serves the chain, the events
   and, later, the genealogies.
4. **Kinship and succession are sourced claims**, like a holder: a parentage can be disputed and keeps both readings.
   **Kinship** (father, mother, siblings) follows the person; **succession** follows the title — the successor of a
   count of Nantes is not his successor as duke of Brittany — and is derived from the titles' dates, not typed.

The certainty of a link is computed from its lines, never typed (`evidence-system.md`, principle of concordance).

## The chain of a place at a date — layer 3 (the dev, 15/11)

**The date is a year** (the dev, 2026-10-30, `../../../../packages/core/src/time/dateOverlap.ts`: *a date alone covers its year — a sheet is a picture of a year*). The place's sheet for that year gives its holder, a person **for a title**; then the link of that title **valid in that
year**, then the suzerain's link, and so on to a title that answers to no one. Nothing is entered here; it is computed.

| case | what the chain does |
| --- | --- |
| a link missing at that date | stops there and says so — *beyond, we do not know*, the third cause of uncertainty, never a silent gap |
| two links diverge | splits; both branches shown — the hatching |
| the holder changes within the year, once or more (Bretislav II dies on 22/12/1100; England in 1066: Edward, Harold II, William) | all, in order, each with its dates — a succession, not a doubt: no hatching. Which polity is painted under a town that changed hands (Barbastro, 18/10/1100) is the drawing's, the dev's |

**Where it lives**: in `core`, pure, tested with literals (`evidence-system.md` §3: the measure shared by the apps).
The CMS shows it under the holder; the Cartographer compares a city with the polity drawn under it through it
(CARTO-435); the atlas, on click, later.

## Reference — the state of the art (read 2026-11-15, kept to what changes a decision today)

The dev's frame: a popularised understanding, enough for a base that is solid and extensible — no survey.

- **It confirms the layers.** The most common model in prosopography is the *factoid* (King's College London; PASE):
  *« un endroit dans une source qui dit quelque chose sur une ou plusieurs personnes »*, two contradictory sources giving
  two factoids — our claims, source lines and contrary readings. The Roman Republic base (DPRR) splits offices, statuses
  and relationships into separate assertions, as our layer 2. Akoka, Comyn-Wattiau & du Mouza, « Conception de bases de
  données prosopographiques en histoire. Un état de l'art », *ROISI* 1(3), 2020, pp. 12–13
  (https://www.openscience.fr/IMG/pdf/iste_roisi20v1n3_1.pdf); Pasin & Bradley 2014, p. 5. *Found.*
- **The one change — identity is a claim.** *"The identity assertion is the work of our historian partners"* (Pasin &
  Bradley, p. 13); telling homonyms apart *« est aujourd'hui encore un problème difficile »* (Akoka, p. 6). So a source
  line keeps **the name as the source writes it** (« Mathieu, comte nantais »), beside the person it is attached to, and
  two records found to be one person are **merged by a signed arbitration**, the reading kept. Cheap now, a migration
  once the lines are entered — part of step 1 bis. *Found.*
- **Left for later, the model already open to it**: a title or a link citing the event that founded it (homage,
  investiture, conquest — Pasin's feudal grant, p. 11); dates relative to another claim (*« durant le règne de
  François Iᵉʳ »*, Akoka p. 9).
