**Réponse** : non norvégienne. L'État libre islandais (*Þjóðveldið*), sans roi ni pouvoir exécutif, de l'Althing (930) à

### L'île de Man (et les Hébrides) — norvégienne en 1100, sous Magnus Berrføtt (vérifié)

**Réponse** : **oui, en 1100 — et plus que vassale**. Magnus III « aux jambes nues » (Berrføtt) prend les Orcades, les
Hébrides et Man en 1098 et y rétablit la **domination norvégienne directe** ; le roi d'Écosse Edgar lui reconnaît les îles
la même année. Il laisse son fils Sigurd, enfant, roi nominal des îles ; à sa mort (23 août 1103) Sigurd rentre en Norvège,
et Lagmann, fils de Godred Crovan, règne avec son accord. **Pour la carte de 1100** : le royaume de Man et des Îles est
tenu par le roi de Norvège en personne (son fils comme roi nominal) — « vassal de la Norvège » est l'approximation la plus
proche ; « sous domination directe » est plus juste.

**Sources** :

  p. 106–109 and 110–113) — **article d'historien** — cited by Wikipedia (en), *Kingdom of the Isles*, for: « Magnus Barefoot had
  re-established direct Norwegian overlordship by 1098 » and for Sigurd « whom he then left in nominal charge of the isles

- Donnchadh Ó Corráin, *Vikings in Ireland and Scotland in the Ninth Century*, CELT, 1998, p. 23 — **article d'historien** —

- Richard Oram, *David I: The King Who Made Scotland*, Stroud, Tempus, 2004, p. 48 — **livre d'historien** — cited by the same page for: « In 1098, Edgar of Scotland
  signed a treaty with Magnus that settled much of the boundary between the Scots and Norwegian claims in the islands. Edgar

- Wikipedia (en), *Magnus Barefoot*: « Mann came under Norwegian control, and Magnus and his men stayed on the island for a

## Venise — quelles villes possède-t-elle en 1100 ?

Asked 2026-11-02. **The answer in one line**: the lagoon (the *Dogado*, from Grado to Loreo) as its own territory, and
**the Dalmatian cities and islands** held since May 1097 in the name of the Byzantine emperor — lost to Coloman of Hungary
in 1105. **Not** Istria (oaths of fidelity since 932 and 1000, possession only from 1145–1150), **not** the trading
quarters (Constantinople 1082, the Levant 1100), which are privileges in others' cities, not territory.

## La Serbie — son nom, ses vassaux, ses villes en 1100 ; le rôle de Byzance ; ce que la Croatie y a perdu

**Réponse** : in 1100 there are **two Serb states, not one**. **Duklja (Dioclea)**, the coastal kingdom of **Constantine
Bodin** (r. 1081–1101), seat at Scutari; and **Serbia** proper — the *Grand Principality of Serbia* (*Velikožupanska
Srbija*; « Raška / Rascia » is a later, anachronistic name) — of the **grand župan Vukan**, **independent of Duklja since
1091 or 1092**. So the answer to « was it Duklja? » is **both, separately**: Duklja had been the Serb state that held
Serbia (1083/84–1091), but by 1100 Serbia is its own master and Duklja is in decline. **Vassals**: Duklja has **none left
for sure** — Raška, Bosnia and Zahumlje broke away in the early 1090s; Serbia has only its own župans (counties), **no
vassal state**. **Byzantium** is the overlord both fight: it captured Bodin, held the frontier (Ras, Lipljan, Skopje), and
made Vukan give hostages in 1094/95. **Croatia** lost **eastern Bosnia** to Bodin in the 1080s, not to Serbia — and Bosnia
was no longer Duklja's in 1100. Certainty: **lu sur la page** (en.wikipedia, read in full through an agent, 2026-11-05),
the decisive line on Serbia's independence cited to **Fine 1991, pp. 224–226**; the Croatian loss **from one historian
only** (Vego 1982), see below.

**For the map** (`years/1100/`, read 2026-11-05): it draws *Kingdom of Duklja* (Q1252942) sovereign, with *Grand Principality
of Serbia* (Q1311990) **as its vassal** — **against Fine**: in 1100 Serbia is **independent** (suzerain: none). The older
map (`years/1100-backup-2026-11-02/`) also gave Duklja Bosnia, Zachlumia and Travunia as vassals — Bosnia and Zachlumia
**against the sources** in 1100; Travunia is **part of Duklja itself**, not a vassal (below). **The dev decides.**

**Wikidata, checked 2026-11-05**: **Q1252942** « Duklja / Dioclée », inception 854, dissolution 1252, P31 historical country

  Vukan, only as a ruler of Serbia » (Kalić 1995, pp. 147–155) — lu sur la page, 2026-11-05 —

- **Names to use for 1100**: « Royaume de Dioclée (Duklja) » and « Grande principauté de Serbie » — Zeta is 12th-century
  later, Raška/Rascie is anachronistic (Wikidata's French label « Rascie » follows the old usage).

### Serbia (Vukan) — independent, no vassal state

- Wikipedia (en), *Vukan*: « He and his brother Marko swore an oath of loyalty to Constantine Bodin and took power as his
  vassals in Serbia in 1083 or 1084 » (Fine p. 223); « In 1091 or 1092, Vukan became independent, taking the title of Grand
  Prince (Veliki Župan) » (Fine p. 225); in 1094–95 « Vukan acted entirely on his own, no longer a vassal of Duklja » (Fine
  p. 226); « Subordinate to him were local dukes (Župan …), more or less autonomous … but obliged to be loyal to Vukan » —
  cited to **John V. A. Fine Jr., *The Early Medieval Balkans*, Ann Arbor: University of Michigan Press, 1991 [1983],
  pp. 223–226** (historian's book) — lu sur la page, 2026-11-05.
- Wikipedia (en), *Grand Principality of Serbia*: the same lines, same Fine pages; infobox « Established 1091 ».
- **Les sources divergent** on the date of independence: « by 1090, during Bodin's captivity » (*Serbia in the Middle
  Ages*, no page) against « 1091 or 1092 » (Fine). Either way, **before 1100**.
- **Marko** « later disappears from sources » (Fine p. 223). No named vassal of Vukan.

### Duklja (Bodin) — its lands and its vassals lost

- Wikipedia (en), *Constantine Bodin*, « Reign »: « he campaigned in Bosnia and Serbia, installing his relative Stephen as
  knez in Bosnia and his nephews Vukan and Marko as župans in Serbia » — after the 14th-century **Chronicle of the Priest of
  Duklja** (late, unreliable), the paragraph cited to **Ivanišević & Krsmanović, « Byzantine Seals from the Ras
  Fortress », *ZRVI* 50 (2013), p. 451** — lu sur la page.
- Wikipedia (en), *Vukan*: « Neither Bosnia, Zachlumia, nor Raška (i.e. Serbia) were ever permanently integrated into the
  Kingdom of Duklja » (Fine p. 223); « Vukan asserts independence, as well as Bosnia and Zahumlje » (Fine p. 224) — lu sur
  la page. **So in 1100 Duklja has no vassal left** — *vérifié* (two pages, one historian, Fine).
- Wikipedia (en), *Duklja*, « Decline » (no footnote): after Bodin's death Dobroslav II, then **Kočopar, sent by the
  Byzantines**, with Vukan's help; then Vladimir « who ruled in peace as a Byzantine vassal ». Bodin's death: « 1101 or
  possibly 1108 » (*Constantine Bodin*, body), « c. 1101 » (*Duklja*) — **les sources divergent**; 1101 is the common date.
- **Travunia (Trebinje) and Zeta are Duklja's own lands, not vassals**: « Travunia was fully incorporated into Doclea »
  (*Travunia*, no footnote — lu sur la page, weak); Bodin crushed the revolt « in the župa of Zeta » by 1085 (*Constantine
  Bodin*, no footnote).

### Le rôle de Byzance

- Bodin, Byzantine vassal by turns: « Having renewed his acknowledgement of Byzantine overlordship, he soon sided with
  their enemies, the Normans, which resulted in a Byzantine invasion and his capture » (*Constantine Bodin*, lead); « In
  1090, they punished Bodin … possibly capturing him for the second time » (*Duklja*, no footnote).
- Serbia: Vukan raided Byzantine Kosovo, burnt Lipljan (1093), withdrew to **Zvečan**; Alexios I came to **Lipljan in
  summer 1094**; Vukan gave hostages (his nephews Uroš and Stefan Vukan and about twenty others — the pages differ on the
  kinship), then broke the peace and took **Vranje, Skopje and Tetovo** for a time (Fine p. 226); war again in 1106, peace
  in November (Fine p. 228). **Ras** stayed contested: « constantly re-captured by the Byzantines until the 1140s »
  (*Serbia in the Middle Ages*, cited to Kalić 1995) — lu sur la page.
- **In 1100 Byzantium is the overlord at the frontier, not the master of the two states**: the theme of Dyrrhachium holds
  Durrës; Ras and Skopje are Byzantine — *vérifié* for Skopje and Lipljan (Fine), *lu* for Ras.

### Ce que la Croatie a perdu

- Wikipedia (en), *Bosnia and Herzegovina in the Middle Ages*: Duklja « after integrating with Raška in the 1070s under
  Constantine Bodin, expanded to conquer **all of eastern Bosnia in the 1080s** » ; « Hungarian authority fell over Bosnia
  in 1102 » — cited to **Marko Vego, 1982, p. 104** (historian; title not captured) — lu sur la page. Bosnia had been
  Croatian before: « it was retaken by Michael Krešimir II of Croatia » after Časlav (10th c.) — **Noel Malcolm, *Bosnia: A
  Short History*, 1994, pp. 9–10** — lu sur la page.
- **So: eastern Bosnia, lost by Croatia to Duklja in the 1080s** — not to Serbia (Vukan's state), and **no longer Duklja's in
  1100** (Fine p. 224). Zahumlje had been under Croatian « influence » in the 10th century (*Zachlumia*), but its passage to
  Duklja is earlier (11th century, dates differ: 1054 infobox, John Vladimir, Vojislav). *Lu sur la page*; **one historian
  (Vego) for the 1080s Bosnian conquest** — a second source would firm it.
- Croatia itself in 1100 is between kings: Petar Snačić killed at Gvozd in 1097, Coloman crowned at Biograd in 1102
  (*Kingdom of Croatia (925–1102)*, lead) — lu sur la page.

## La frontière France / Saint-Empire — les villes-témoins (05–06/11)

Asked 2026-11-05 evening: *"find every border town between France and the Holy Roman Empire in 1100 and enter them"*, so
the dev can replace the long straight lines of his map. **Everything is in the CMS** (sources 19–68, place-periods
114–165 and 55 Arles), each holder with its source line, page and quotation; this section keeps the reasoning.

### The method — towns that face each other (the dev's rule, 2026-11-06)

A town **on** the line (Ghent, Menin/Mouscron today) does not help: nobody knows which side to put it. What draws the
border is a **pair of towns facing each other**, one on each side, 5–30 km from the line, each with a sourced holder;
the line passes between them. Turned into a Cartographer feature: **CARTO-366** (Backlog) — a suggested line halfway
between towns of different holders, accepted or retouched by the dev; rivers named by a source (Scheldt, Saône) later.

### The line in 1100, as the sources draw it (vérifié unless said)

- **North, the Scheldt** — Flanders west of it in the kingdom, Imperial Flanders (Alost, Grammont, the Four Offices)
  east of it since 1046/1056 (Longnon p. 215–216, 223–224; Pirenne p. 94–95). The **Ostrevant** (Bouchain, Douai) is a
  French fief held by the imperial count of Hainaut (Longnon p. 222; Vanderkindere in Gislebert p. 35 n.).
- **The Meuse and the Argonne** — the Meuse is the line at Mézières (*« Die Maas ist Grenzfluss bei Mezières »*,
  Menke p. 36, 1006); Mouzon, Donchery and Dun are imperial; the Astenois (Sainte-Menehould) is French (Menke p. 36);
  between them the line runs **through the Argonne**, Sainte-Menehould (France) facing Clermont-en-Argonne (Empire).
  Bar is imperial until the homage of 1301 (Longnon p. 250).
- **The Saône** — the line down to Mâcon (*« fluvium Arar qui nunc temporis dividit regnum ab imperio »*, Poupardin
  p. 180 n. 7, early 12th c.); Bresse and the county of Burgundy (Dole, Auxonne, Seurre, Cuisery, Bâgé) imperial.
- **South of Mâcon the Rhône is NOT the line**: the Lyonnais, the Forez and the Vivarais are imperial (Longnon p. 216,
  223; Fournier p. VII), so the line runs along their **western edge** — no facing pair found there.
- **The Rhône delta** — two Provençal (imperial) strips on the right bank: the Argence (Beaucaire, Fourques) and
  Saze–Tavel–Lirac–Saint-Laurent-des-Arbres–Montfaucon; Aramon and Vallabrègues are left to the French Uzège; the line
  passes *« devant Saint-Gilles »* (Manteyer p. 165–167).

- **Barbastro and Balaguer change hands in late 1100**: drawn as they stand on 1 January 1100 (Muslim), or as on 31

## La Navarre et l'Aragon — les villes de l'union de 1076–1134 (06/11)

Asked 2026-11-06: *"the same work for Aragon and Navarre"*, from the maps of en.wikipedia « Kingdom of Navarre ». Only one of
them names towns: **Commons, *Reino de Navarra - La union con Aragón*** (Miguillen 2009, CC BY-SA 3.0), which declares its
source — Pérez Equiza, *Atlas de Navarra*, Gobierno de Navarra 2006 — and shows 1076–1134 with conquest dates. The page's other
maps carry no town names (*Reparto … Sancho IV*: 11 towns, all on the union map). **In the CMS since 2026-11-06**: sources
175–283, places 221–295 (Saragosse 49 reused), place-periods 1100 213–287, Saragosse's sheet (41) given its holder. Left out,
no source for 1100: Herrera de los Navarros, Galve, Gúdar; Monreal del Campo was founded 1120–1124. Gascon and Castilian
towns of the map (Bayonne … Sigüenza) are outside the lot. Quotations were checked against the downloaded texts (171 of 204
exact; the rest OCR — Lacarra 1975, Moret, Yanguas, Zurita — read by eye; three not found and not entered).

### The polities in 1100

- **Peter I, king of Aragon and Pamplona (1094–1104)** holds Navarre, Aragon, Sobrarbe and Ribagorza. The towns are entered
  under the **Kingdom of Pamplona** (Q3446210) or the **Kingdom of Aragon** (Q199442) as the sources place them — one king.
- **The County of Navarre** (Pamplona, Estella, Falces, Ujué…, Q5781820) was held under homage to Alfonso VI (Lacarra p. 73;
  GEN « Sancho Sánchez »): a vassal tier inside the kingdom, entered as lines, not as the sheet's holder.
- **León-Castile (Alfonso VI, Q179293)** holds the Rioja (count García Ordóñez, 1076–1108), Álava and Vizcaya (lords of
  Biscay, Q3045810).
- **The taifa of Zaragoza (al-Musta'in II, Q33061)** holds the Ebro: Tudela, Tarazona, Borja, Ejea, Tauste, Zaragoza,
  Calatayud, Daroca, Belchite. **Lleida** (Q2166417) the lower Cinca and Segre. **Tortosa**: the Almoravids (Q75613), three
  witnesses against two (see below). **Molina** (Q2232777) and **Albarracín** (Q2060937) in the south.
