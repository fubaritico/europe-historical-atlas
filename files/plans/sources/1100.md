# The sources of 1100 — one question at a time

> The historian instance's file for the map of 1100 (`files/plans/maps-1100-1200-code-and-historian.md`). One section per
> question the dev asks, newest at the bottom: the answer, its certainty (*lu sur la page* / *vérifié* / *de mémoire*), and
> one line per source — the work at the end of the chain of reference, its kind, the quotation, where it was read, the date.
> Sources that copy one another are one *témoin* (`files/plans/evidence-system.md`, principle 3). A divergence is kept, every
> answer with its sources: the dev arbitrates. These entries go into the CMS once CMS-338 exists.

## L'Islande, le Groenland et l'île de Man — appartiennent-ils à la Norvège en 1100 ?

Asked 2026-11-02, from a Wikipedia page listing them as Norwegian.

**Where that claim comes from**: Wikipedia (en), *Kingdom of Norway (872–1397)*, section « Overseas — Crown dependencies »,
lists « Ísland (Iceland) · Grǿnland (Greenland) · Færeyjar · Mann (Isle of Man) · Hjaltland · Orkneyjar · Suðreyjar »
**without any date and without a reference** — a list of the realm at its widest (after 1261–1264), not a state of 1100.
Read 2026-11-02 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Norway_(872%E2%80%931397). **Not a source for 1100.**

### L'Islande — indépendante en 1100 (vérifié)

**Réponse** : non norvégienne. L'État libre islandais (*Þjóðveldið*), sans roi ni pouvoir exécutif, de l'Althing (930) à
l'« Ancien pacte » (*Gamli sáttmáli*) de 1262–1264, par lequel les chefs prêtent serment au roi de Norvège.

**Sources** :
- Sverrir Jakobsson, « All the King's men. The incorporation of Iceland into the Norwegian Realm », *Scandinavian Journal of
  History* 46 (5), 2021, p. 571–592 — **article d'historien** — cited by Wikipedia (en), *Icelandic Commonwealth*, for:
  « The end of the Icelandic Commonwealth is typically dated to the Old Covenant (1262–1264) or to the adoption of Jónsbók in
  1281 » and « During the 13th century, Iceland came under the control of the Kingdom of Norway » — lu sur la page (the
  reference, not the article: paywalled), 2026-11-02 — https://doi.org/10.1080/03468755.2021.1961856
- Gunnar Karlsson, *The History of Iceland*, 2000, p. 82–83 — **livre d'historien** — cited by
  the same page for: « By 1264, all Icelandic chieftains had sworn allegiance to the King of Norway » — lu sur la page,
  2026-11-02 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_Commonwealth
- Wikipedia (en), *Icelandic Commonwealth*, lead, **no reference on the sentence**: « the political unit existing in Iceland
  between the establishment of the Althing (Alþingi) in 930 and the pledge of fealty to the Norwegian king with the Old
  Covenant in 1262 » — lu sur la page, 2026-11-02.

**The map today** (read 2026-11-02): `years/1100/` (Cliopatria) draws *Icelandic Commonwealth*, no suzerain — in agreement.
The dev's own 1100 (`years/1100.dev-2026-11-01/`) drew Iceland **inside *Kingdom of Norway*** (its rings reach lon −45, so
Greenland too) — against the sources.

### Le Groenland — indépendant en 1100 (vérifié)

**Réponse** : non norvégien. Les établissements norrois (Eystribyggð, Vestribyggð) ont leur assemblée, leur *lagmann* et
leurs lois ; ils se soumettent au roi Håkon Håkonsson en **1261**. L'évêché de Garðar, rattaché à Nidaros, n'est fondé qu'en
1126 — après 1100 (*lu sur la page*, *History of Greenland* ; un rattachement d'abord à Lund est *de mémoire*, non vérifié).

**Sources** :
- Susan Barr, Per G. Norseng, Nils Petter Thuesen, « Grønlands historie », *Store norske leksikon* (updated 2026-01-19) —
  **encyclopédie signée** — « I 1261 ble Grønland (d.e. de norrøne bygdene) ifølge en avtale med den norske kongen Håkon
  Håkonsson formelt knyttet til Norgesveldet » ; before: « ordnet på samme vis som de norrøne samfunnene på de øvrige
  Vesterhavsøyene, med ting, lagmann og egen lovgivning » — lu sur la page (read through a summarising fetch: the quotation
  to be re-read by eye), 2026-11-02 — https://snl.no/Grønlands_historie
- Wikipedia (en), *History of Greenland*, **no reference on the sentence**: « In 1261 the population accepted the over
  lordship of the King of Norway, although it continued to have its own law » ; and « In 1126 the Roman Catholic Church
  founded a diocese at Garðar » — lu sur la page, 2026-11-02 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Greenland
- Wikipedia (fr), *Histoire du Groenland*, **no reference**: « 1261 : le Groenland reconnaît la souveraineté norvégienne en
  échange de l'assurance d'être visité par deux bateaux par an » — lu sur la page, 2026-11-02.
- Wikipedia (en), *List of Greenlandic rulers*, **no reference**: « The Norse Colony of Greenland (982–1261) · The Kingdom of
  Norway (1261–1814) » — lu sur la page, 2026-11-02.
- **Not reached**: the judgment of the Permanent Court of International Justice, *Legal Status of Eastern Greenland*
  (1933, Series A/B no. 53), which states the 1261 submission (a search summary quotes « In 1261, the Greenlanders submitted
  themselves of their own free will to the King of Norway ») — icj-cij.org refused the download (403). To be read.

**Les sources divergent, un peu** : one search result gives 1262 for the formal attachment; every page read gives 1261.
Not a question for 1100 — either date is after it.

**The three Wikipedia sentences and the SNL are probably one *témoin*** for the date; SNL is the one signed.

**The map today** (read 2026-11-02): `years/1100/` (Cliopatria) draws nothing west of lon −24.4 — no Greenland at all. The
dev's own 1100 drew it inside *Kingdom of Norway* — against the sources. If Greenland is drawn, it is its own polity (*Norse
Greenland*), no suzerain.

### Le Groenland norrois — jusqu'où s'étend-il en 1100 ? Les villes-témoins

Asked 2026-11-02: the southernmost and the north-westernmost Norse sites, to set the extent. **There are no towns** in
Norse Greenland: farms and churches in two settlements, the Eastern (Eystribyggð, around 60–61° N) and the Western
(Vestribyggð, around 64° N; Herjólfsnes to Sandnæs is 551 km as the crow flies, computed from the coordinates below). The *villes-témoins* are therefore farms.

**Certainty**: Herjólfsnes as the south is *vérifié* (two pages agree; the map of the Eastern Settlement shows it
southernmost). **Sandnæs is the largest and best-known farm of the north-west, not proven the northernmost**: the
Western Settlement's farms run up to the inland ice along Nuup Kangerlua and Ameralik (*de mémoire*, not read); the
survey of its ~95 ruins that would give the outermost one was not reached (ScienceDirect and ResearchGate refused, 403) —
*à chercher* if the line must be exact. **Not territory**: the hunting grounds of the Norðrsetur (Disko Bay) and the
Kingittorsuaq runestone (~73° N, a search summary, later than 1100) — seasonal reach, not occupation.

**For the map**: the Norse occupation is **two coastal pockets on the south-west coast** (60–61° N and 64° N), not the
island; the ice and the rest of the coast belong to no polity in 1100 (the Thule Inuit arrive from the north-west later,
*de mémoire*).

### L'île de Man (et les Hébrides) — norvégienne en 1100, sous Magnus Berrføtt (vérifié)

**Réponse** : **oui, en 1100 — et plus que vassale**. Magnus III « aux jambes nues » (Berrføtt) prend les Orcades, les
Hébrides et Man en 1098 et y rétablit la **domination norvégienne directe** ; le roi d'Écosse Edgar lui reconnaît les îles
la même année. Il laisse son fils Sigurd, enfant, roi nominal des îles ; à sa mort (23 août 1103) Sigurd rentre en Norvège,
et Lagmann, fils de Godred Crovan, règne avec son accord. **Pour la carte de 1100** : le royaume de Man et des Îles est
tenu par le roi de Norvège en personne (son fils comme roi nominal) — « vassal de la Norvège » est l'approximation la plus
proche ; « sous domination directe » est plus juste.

**Sources** :
- Seán Duffy, « Irishmen and Islesmen in the Kingdom of Dublin and Man 1052–1171 », *Ériu* 43, 1992, p. 93–133 (cited
  p. 106–109 and 110–113) — **article d'historien** — cited by Wikipedia (en), *Kingdom of the Isles*, for: « Magnus Barefoot had
  re-established direct Norwegian overlordship by 1098 » and for Sigurd « whom he then left in nominal charge of the isles
  […] On 23 August 1103 Magnus was killed fighting in Ulster and the 14-year-old Sigurd returned to Norway » — lu sur la
  page, 2026-11-02 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Isles
- Donnchadh Ó Corráin, *Vikings in Ireland and Scotland in the Ninth Century*, CELT, 1998, p. 23 — **article d'historien** —
  cited for the same sentence on 1098 — lu sur la page (the reference, from the page's bibliography), 2026-11-02.
- Richard Oram, *David I: The King Who Made Scotland*, Stroud, Tempus, 2004, p. 48 — **livre d'historien** — cited by the same page for: « In 1098, Edgar of Scotland
  signed a treaty with Magnus that settled much of the boundary between the Scots and Norwegian claims in the islands. Edgar
  formally acknowledged the existing situation by giving up his claims to the Hebrides and Kintyre » — lu sur la page,
  2026-11-02.
- Wikipedia (en), *Magnus Barefoot*: « Mann came under Norwegian control, and Magnus and his men stayed on the island for a
  time » (no reference on the sentence); infobox: King of the Isles 1098–1102, successor Sigurd I; Sigurd named co-king,
  cited Førsund, *Magnus Berrføtt*, 2012, p. 122–123 — lu sur la page (read through a summarising fetch), 2026-11-02 —
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Barefoot
- *The Chronicle of Man and the Sudreys* (13th c., ed. 1874), p. 51–55 — **document d'époque** (a chronicle written later,
  in the 13th century), cited by *Kingdom of the Isles* for the battle of Santwat (1098) and Lagmann's reign — lu sur la page
  (the reference), 2026-11-02.

**Les sources divergent** : (1) Oram (2011, *Domination and Lordship*, p. 49–50, cited by *Magnus Barefoot*) calls the
treaty with Edgar, as the sagas tell it, a « post-Norwegian civil war confection » — the 1098 conquest is not in doubt, the
terms of the treaty are. (2) *Kingdom of the Isles*, note: « Some sources have Lagmann reigning before Magnus's expedition
and being deposed by him » (Anderson, 1922, p. 108) — changes who was king of Man before 1098, not who held it in 1100.

**The map today**: `years/1100/` (Cliopatria) has *Kingdom of the Isles* with suzerain *Old Kingdom of Norway*; the dev's own
1100 had *Kingdom of Mann and the Isles* vassal of *Kingdom of Norway*. Both in agreement with the sources.

## Venise — quelles villes possède-t-elle en 1100 ?

Asked 2026-11-02. **The answer in one line**: the lagoon (the *Dogado*, from Grado to Loreo) as its own territory, and
**the Dalmatian cities and islands** held since May 1097 in the name of the Byzantine emperor — lost to Coloman of Hungary
in 1105. **Not** Istria (oaths of fidelity since 932 and 1000, possession only from 1145–1150), **not** the trading
quarters (Constantinople 1082, the Levant 1100), which are privileges in others' cities, not territory.

### L'Istrie et les comptoirs — pas des possessions en 1100

- Wikipedia (en), *History of Istria*: under Doge Pietro II Candiano (932–939) « coastal cities of Istria signed a first act
  of devotion to the Venetian rule »; in 1000 the Istrian and Dalmatian cities « swore an oath of fidelity to Venice »,
  cited to *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani* (Treccani), « Orseolo, Pietro II »; « In 1145, the cities of Pula, Koper
  and Izola rose against the Republic » — lu sur la page, 2026-11-02 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Istria.
  **In 1100 Istria belongs to the March of Istria (Holy Roman Empire)**, its coastal cities bound to Venice by oaths, not
  held — *de mémoire* for the March; the oaths are read.
- Wikipedia (en), *Vitale I Michiel*: the 1099–1100 expedition to the Levant gives Venice « the right to maintain a quarter
  not subject to custom offices, taxes, or excise taxes in every conquered city » — a privilege, not a territory; the page
  carries a « refimprove » banner — lu sur la page, 2026-11-02.
