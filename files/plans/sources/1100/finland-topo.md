## The short answer

In 1100 there is **no state, no kingdom and no town** in what is now Finland. There are **three Baltic-Finnic peoples with
their own lands** (Finns Proper in the south-west, Tavastians in the inland south, Karelians on the Karelian Isthmus and
the western shore of Lake Ladoga), the **Sámi** (and, in the sources, the **Kvens / Cwenas**) to the north, and two
powers that are *neighbours, not rulers*: **Novgorod** to the east (a loose link with the Karelians, raids on the Yem)
and **Sweden** to the west (Swedish Crusades only from the mid-12th century on, and the first known bishop in the early
13th). The Swedish crown does not hold anything in Finland in 1100.

**Neighbours.** East: **Novgorod** (Ladoga as its port: Staraïa Ladoga is in the base, place 1555), the **Vod** (Votians,
attacked by the Yem in 1149), the **Izhora**, the **Chud** (Estonians), and the **Livonians** farther south. West: the
Swedes (Gs 13 shows a Swedish expedition or levy in Tavastia in the 11th century, which is not rule). North and
north-east: the Sámi, the Kvens, the Biarmians (Ohthere).

## The places

- **No town is attested before 1105 in this area.** Turku is 13th century; Käkisalmi/Korela is first named in the
  Chronicle in 1314 as a *town* (the people are named in 1143) and is dated to the 12th century by archaeology (SEEN);
  neither meets the criterion of a source attesting the place **at** the date.
- **Hillforts of Tavastia** (Rapola, the largest in Finland; Hakoinen; Tenhola), the Iron Age line around Hämeenlinna:
  **archaeology only** — the dev accepted "archaeology alone" as a source on 14/11 (Poland). Their datings are **not read**
  yet (SEEN on the Wikipedia page "Tavastians" only).
- In the base: **Staraïa Ladoga** (1555) and **Novgorod** (79); territory 261, *Principality of Novgorod*. Nothing for
  Finland. The dev's map: Finland without a country (consistent with the above); the isthmus of Karelia
  (Vyborg, Käkisalmi) drawn under Novgorod — **the sources say "loosely linked, relatively independent" (Lind),
  not "ruled"**; no source for 1100 either way.

## What is not settled

- **Who the *Yem* are.** Tavastians is the usual identification (the name is *Häme*), but the chronicles' term "probably
  referred to several different groups" (search result on *Finnish–Novgorodian wars*, not checked at the source). An
  entry saying "Yem" is not evidence for *Tavastians* alone.
- **The date of the 1042 campaign.** The scan's year table is scrambled; the entry sits in the block 1042–1045.
- **Whether any Finnish people had a ruler in 1100.** The sources show *groups acting together* (a levy, a raid), never a
  named ruler. The chronicle names no Yem prince; Gs 13 names no Tavastian leader.
