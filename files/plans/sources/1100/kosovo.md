## For the dev's map — the border runs at Zvečan, not across the plain

The 1100 map gives the Grand Principality of Serbia (Q1311990) the north-west of Kosovo — Peć, Dečani, Gjakova,
Vučitrn, Mitrovica, Leposavić, Banjska. The sources:

| area | the map | the sources | witnesses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hvostno** — the basin of the White Drin around Peć and Dečani, with Gjakova | Serbia | **Byzantine**: part of the see of Prizren, passed to Nemanja only at the peace of 1190 | Jireček 1911, p. 374 (1190, kept as it shows the state before); Jireček on the sees of Prizren, Lipljan and Ras after 1018; Komatina 2012 |
| **The plain of Kosovo** (Amselfeld, the Sitnica and the Lab), Vučitrn included | Serbia in its north half | **Byzantine**: the whole Amselfeld passed to Serbia in 1190; the Byzantine border camp at Lipljan | Jireček pp. 238–239 and 374; the *Alexiad* IX, 4 and 10 |
| **The Ibar valley north of the plain** — Zvečan, Mitrovica, Leposavić, Banjska | Serbia | **Serbian**: Zvečan, Vukan's border fortress | Jireček p. 238; the *Alexiad* IX, 4 |

**Readings kept against this**:
- Stephenson (*Byzantium's Balkan Frontier*, 2000, pp. 125, 149–150) puts **Zvečan** among the Byzantine watchtowers,
  in a disputed land (*mesaichmion*) between Lipljan and Zvečan, and reports Popović's view that Alexios I rebuilt it.
- **Peć** lay on the line Ras – Zvečan – Peć beyond which the Serbs raided under Alexios I (Jireček): a Serbian hold
  on the upper Hvostno cannot be excluded, though no witness gives it.
- **Duklja** over Metohija: searched in Jireček and Stephenson, no witness.

The 1190 witness is an event after the date: it says what Serbia gained then, so what it did not hold before. The
plain was a war zone in the 1090s: hatching (the belonging is uncertain) is a fair reading for the strip between
Lipljan and Zvečan.
